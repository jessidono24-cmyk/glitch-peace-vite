# Architecture Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 10)

### [Alexander et al., 1977] — A Pattern Language
**Full citation:** Alexander, C., Ishikawa, S., & Silverstein, M. (1977). *A Pattern Language: Towns, Buildings, Construction*. Oxford University Press.
**Key finding:** 253 architectural patterns describe recurring solutions to human needs; human comfort correlates with spaces that match deeply felt structural needs (light, scale, threshold).
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — building pattern rewards.
**Application:** Buildings in Architecture mode score higher when they incorporate recognized patterns from Alexander's taxonomy (courtyards, alcoves, light on two sides), training players in human-centered design.

### [Lawlor, 1982] — Sacred Geometry
**Full citation:** Lawlor, R. (1982). *Sacred Geometry: Philosophy and Practice*. Thames and Hudson.
**Key finding:** Cross-cultural architectural traditions (Egyptian, Greek, Gothic, Islamic) independently derived the same proportional systems — golden ratio, √2, √3 — as structurally and aesthetically optimal.
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — golden-ratio proportion bonus.
**Application:** Structures whose proportions approach φ (1.618…) receive an aesthetic-harmony bonus, and structures approximating integer multiples of √2 receive a structural-integrity bonus, based on Lawlor's analysis.

### [Vitruvius, c.25 BCE] — De Architectura
**Full citation:** Vitruvius Pollio, M. (c.25 BCE). *De Architectura*. (Trans. Morgan, M.H. (1914). *Vitruvius: The Ten Books on Architecture*. Harvard University Press. — Public domain.)
**Key finding:** Architecture must satisfy three principles simultaneously: *firmitas* (structural soundness), *utilitas* (utility/function), and *venustas* (beauty/delight).
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — three-attribute scoring system.
**Application:** Every structure is scored on three axes — Strength, Utility, and Beauty — directly implementing Vitruvius's triad. Maximum score requires all three to be optimized, preventing players from gaming any single metric.

### [Pallasmaa, 2005] — The Eyes of the Skin
**Full citation:** Pallasmaa, J. (2005). *The Eyes of the Skin: Architecture and the Senses*. Wiley-Academy.
**Key finding:** Architecture is experienced through all senses simultaneously; the dominance of vision in modern design has impoverished spatial experience and embodied cognition.
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — multi-sensory building bonuses.
**Application:** Players can add sonic resonance tiles, texture tiles, and scent-note tiles to buildings; multi-sensory structures score a holistic-experience multiplier, rewarding Pallasmaa's multi-modal design philosophy.

### [Piaget, 1952] — The Origins of Intelligence in Children
**Full citation:** Piaget, J. (1952). *The Origins of Intelligence in Children*. International Universities Press.
**Key finding:** Knowledge is constructed through active engagement with material; children learn spatial-structural concepts by building and modifying physical structures (constructivism).
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — learn-by-building knowledge construction.
**Application:** The game teaches structural principles (load distribution, cantilever limits) not through instruction but through building and observing collapse — a direct application of Piagetian constructivism.

### [Ching, 1979] — Architecture: Form, Space, and Order
**Full citation:** Ching, F.D.K. (1979). *Architecture: Form, Space, and Order*. Van Nostrand Reinhold.
**Key finding:** All architectural form derives from a small vocabulary of primitive elements (point, line, plane, volume) and their relational operations (subtraction, addition, rotation).
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — tile vocabulary.
**Application:** Building tiles are organized by Ching's primitive vocabulary: foundation tiles (plane), wall tiles (line), corner tiles (point), dome tiles (volume) — making the underlying grammar of architecture legible.

### [Sennett, 2008] — The Craftsman
**Full citation:** Sennett, R. (2008). *The Craftsman*. Yale University Press.
**Key finding:** Deep skill in making — craftsmanship — requires 10,000 hours of engaged, reflective practice; tacit knowledge accumulates through repetition with feedback.
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — mastery progression.
**Application:** Architecture mode tracks a "craft level" (distinct from emergence level) that accumulates only through building and revising; higher craft levels unlock advanced structural types, rewarding Sennett's reflective practice.

### [Hall, 1966] — The Hidden Dimension
**Full citation:** Hall, E.T. (1966). *The Hidden Dimension*. Doubleday.
**Key finding:** Proxemics identifies four culturally modulated spatial zones (intimate, personal, social, public); spatial comfort is violated when these are transgressed.
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — human-scale spacing requirements.
**Application:** Populated buildings require that internal spaces conform to proxemic zones for their inhabitants; overcrowding triggers "spatial stress" and degrades utility score, reflecting Hall's proxemic theory.

### [Norberg-Schulz, 1980] — Genius Loci
**Full citation:** Norberg-Schulz, C. (1980). *Genius Loci: Towards a Phenomenology of Architecture*. Rizzoli.
**Key finding:** Every place has a spirit (*genius loci*) arising from its particular combination of natural landscape, light, and built form; authentic architecture responds to this spirit.
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — biome-responsive design bonus.
**Application:** Structures that incorporate the predominant materials, lighting angles, and form-language of their dreamscape biome receive a Genius Loci bonus, rewarding contextual sensitivity over generic design.

### [Rossi, 1982] — The Architecture of the City
**Full citation:** Rossi, A. (1982). *The Architecture of the City*. MIT Press. (Original: L'Architettura della Città, 1966.)
**Key finding:** Cities accumulate memory in their physical fabric; monuments persist as collective memory anchors that give urban identity across time.
**Game mechanic:** Architecture mode — landmark placement scoring.
**Application:** Designated landmark buildings in each dreamscape act as memory anchors; placing new buildings within their sight lines and at appropriate scale earns a Rossi-inspired urban-memory bonus.
