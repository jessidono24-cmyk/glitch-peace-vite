# Chronobiology Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 10)

### [Kleitman, 1963] — Sleep and Wakefulness
**Full citation:** Kleitman, N. (1963). *Sleep and Wakefulness* (rev. ed.). University of Chicago Press.
**Key finding:** The basic rest-activity cycle (BRAC) operates in ~90-minute ultradian cycles during both sleep and waking; performance peaks and troughs follow this rhythm.
**Game mechanic:** Ultradian session design — 90-minute session arc.
**Application:** The game's optimal session length is calibrated to one ultradian cycle; sessions beginning at BRAC trough (lower alertness) receive an easier opening difficulty ramp, matching Kleitman's cycle.

### [Refinetti, 2006] — Circadian Physiology
**Full citation:** Refinetti, R. (2006). *Circadian Physiology* (2nd ed.). CRC Press.
**Key finding:** Virtually all physiological systems operate on endogenous ~24-hour circadian clocks; peak cognitive performance typically occurs in mid-to-late morning for most chronotypes.
**Game mechanic:** Daily challenge unlock timing.
**Application:** Daily challenges unlock at chronotype-adjusted times (configurable: early bird vs. night owl), ensuring the game's hardest content is presented at each player's personal cognitive peak.

### [Walker, 2017] — Why We Sleep
**Full citation:** Walker, M.P. (2017). *Why We Sleep: Unlocking the Power of Sleep and Dreams*. Scribner.
**Key finding:** Sleep, especially REM sleep, consolidates procedural and emotional memory; patterns learned before sleep are more robustly retained after sleep.
**Game mechanic:** Dream integration bonus — post-sleep session reward.
**Application:** A "dream bonus" applies when the player returns to the game after a full night's sleep, rewarding the memory consolidation Walker documents and encouraging healthy sleep habits.

### [Borbély, 1982] — A Two-Process Model of Sleep Regulation
**Full citation:** Borbély, A.A. (1982). A two process model of sleep regulation. *Human Neurobiology*, 1(3), 195–204.
**Key finding:** Sleep is regulated by two processes: Process S (homeostatic sleep pressure, rising during wake) and Process C (circadian alerting signal); their interaction determines alertness.
**Game mechanic:** Session fatigue modeling.
**Application:** The game tracks session length and time of day; after extended play, a "fatigue indicator" rises (Process S), nudging players to rest — modeling Borbély's homeostatic sleep pressure.

### [Czeisler et al., 1999] — Stability, Precision, and Near-24-Hour Period of the Human Circadian Pacemaker
**Full citation:** Czeisler, C.A., Duffy, J.F., Shanahan, T.L., Brown, E.N., Mitchell, J.F., Rimmer, D.W., … Kronauer, R.E. (1999). Stability, precision, and near-24-hour period of the human circadian pacemaker. *Science*, 284(5423), 2177–2181.
**Key finding:** The human endogenous circadian period averages ~24.18 hours and is entrained daily by light; disruption leads to measurable cognitive impairment.
**Game mechanic:** Planetary hour system — daily gameplay timing.
**Application:** The planetary hour system aligns game events with traditional astrological timing, which maps loosely onto the 24-hour light-dark cycle that Czeisler established as the primary circadian zeitgeber.

### [Pittendrigh, 1960] — Circadian Rhythms and the Circadian Organization of Living Systems
**Full citation:** Pittendrigh, C.S. (1960). Circadian rhythms and the circadian organization of living systems. *Cold Spring Harbor Symposia on Quantitative Biology*, 25, 159–184.
**Key finding:** Circadian clocks are endogenously generated, temperature-compensated oscillators that evolved to anticipate environmental cycles, not merely react to them.
**Game mechanic:** Predictive event scheduling.
**Application:** Game events scheduled by the planetary system are delivered in advance of the day (evening notification), honoring Pittendrigh's finding that biological timing is anticipatory, not reactive.

### [Monk et al., 1992] — Social Rhythm and Psychiatric Vulnerability
**Full citation:** Monk, T.H., Flaherty, J.F., Frank, E., Hoskinson, K., & Kupfer, D.J. (1992). The Social Rhythm Metric: An instrument measuring the daily rhythms of life. *Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease*, 180(3), 189–194.
**Key finding:** Social rhythm regularity (consistent daily activity timing) is protective against mood disorder relapse; irregularity is a key vulnerability factor.
**Game mechanic:** Daily session streak rewards.
**Application:** The streak system rewards consistent daily play at similar times, directly supporting social rhythm regularity as a mood-stabilizing protective factor Monk et al. document.

### [Roenneberg, 2012] — Internal Time
**Full citation:** Roenneberg, T. (2012). *Internal Time: Chronotypes, Social Jet Lag, and Why You're So Tired*. Harvard University Press.
**Key finding:** Chronotype (morning vs. evening preference) is biologically determined and shifts later during adolescence, returning to earlier timing in midlife; social jet lag harms health.
**Game mechanic:** Chronotype-adjusted difficulty curve.
**Application:** Players set their chronotype during onboarding; the game adjusts daily difficulty ramps so that peak challenge aligns with each player's personal alertness peak, reducing social jet lag effect.

### [Spiegel et al., 2004] — Sleep Curtailment Is Accompanied by Increased Levels of Ghrelin
**Full citation:** Spiegel, K., Tasali, E., Penev, P., & Van Cauter, E. (2004). Brief communication: Sleep curtailment in healthy young men is associated with decreased leptin levels, elevated ghrelin levels, and increased hunger and appetite. *Annals of Internal Medicine*, 141(11), 846–850.
**Key finding:** Sleep loss elevates appetite-stimulating hormones (ghrelin) and reduces satiety hormones (leptin), increasing vulnerability to impulsive overconsumption.
**Game mechanic:** Post-sleep-deprivation impulse buffer extension.
**Application:** If the player has set a "short sleep" flag (< 6 hours), the impulse buffer before RAGE/DESPAIR tiles is extended, compensating for the reduced inhibitory control Spiegel et al. document.

### [Dunlap, 1999] — Molecular Bases for Circadian Clocks
**Full citation:** Dunlap, J.C. (1999). Molecular bases for circadian clocks. *Cell*, 96(2), 271–290.
**Key finding:** Circadian clocks are built from transcription-translation feedback loops (TTFL) conserved across eukaryotes; the CLOCK/BMAL1 loop is the master pacemaker in mammals.
**Game mechanic:** Circadian mode — planetary clock visualization.
**Application:** The planetary clock visualization uses a TTFL-inspired loop animation (two interlocking cycles representing CLOCK and BMAL1), grounding the esoteric planetary-hour system in real molecular oscillator science.
