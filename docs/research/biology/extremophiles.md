# Extremophiles & Difficulty Adaptation

## Sources
- Rothschild, L.J. & Mancinelli, R.L. (2001). "Life in extreme environments." *Nature*, 409(6823), 1092-1101.
- Horikoshi, K. & Grant, W.D. (1998). *Extremophiles: Microbial Life in Extreme Environments*. Wiley-Liss.
- Meyers, M.A. et al. (2008). "Biological materials: Structure and mechanical properties." *Progress in Materials Science*, 53(1), 1-206.

## Key Findings
1. Extremophiles (heat, cold, acid, pressure, radiation tolerant organisms) achieve survival through **structural adaptation**, not just endurance
2. **Stress hardening**: Brief exposure to sub-lethal stressors often increases organism's tolerance (hormesis)
3. Extremophiles are never "generalists" — extreme specialization enables extreme performance in a specific niche
4. Cross-tolerance: Organisms adapted to one extreme often develop tolerance to other extremes
5. Recovery from extreme stress requires adequate resource availability — starved organisms cannot adapt

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- Dynamic difficulty system mirrors hormesis: brief hard challenges followed by easier recovery periods
- "Extremophile" difficulty tier: reward system for players who successfully navigate high-stress scenarios
- Cross-mode training: mastery in one mode should provide measurable bonus in other modes (cross-tolerance)
- Recovery mechanic: after high-difficulty session, reduced difficulty window for consolidation
