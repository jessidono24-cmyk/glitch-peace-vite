# Chemistry Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 10)

### [Jung, 1944] — Psychology and Alchemy
**Full citation:** Jung, C.G. (1944). *Psychologie und Alchemie* [Psychology and Alchemy]. Rascher Verlag. (Translated: Princeton University Press, 1968.)
**Key finding:** Alchemical transformation symbols (nigredo, albedo, citrinitas, rubedo) are projections of psychological individuation stages — alchemy as depth psychology.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — transmutation stages.
**Application:** The four alchemical stages map to the game's four transmutation tiers: nigredo (dissolution/dark tiles), albedo (purification/white tiles), citrinitas (illumination/gold tiles), rubedo (integration/red-gold tiles).

### [Paracelsus, c.1530] — Opus Paramirum
**Full citation:** Paracelsus. (c.1530). *Opus Paramirum*. (Translated in: Jacobi, J. (1951). *Paracelsus: Selected Writings*. Princeton University Press.)
**Key finding:** The three primes — sulfur (soul/consciousness), mercury (spirit/process), salt (body/matter) — underlie all chemical transformation; disease is imbalance of these principles.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — three-element combination system.
**Application:** The three core element types (Sulfur, Mercury, Salt) in the Alchemy mode combine according to Paracelsian rules; correct combinations produce transmutation; incorrect combinations create instability tiles.

### [Principe, 2013] — The Secrets of Alchemy
**Full citation:** Principe, L.M. (2013). *The Secrets of Alchemy*. University of Chicago Press.
**Key finding:** Medieval alchemists performed genuine laboratory chemistry; many procedures were rational attempts at material transformation, not pure symbolism.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — empirical combination rules.
**Application:** Combination rules in Alchemy mode are derived from actual historical laboratory procedures (distillation, calcination, sublimation), grounding the mystical aesthetic in real historical practice as Principe documents.

### [Dobbs, 1975] — The Foundations of Newton's Alchemy
**Full citation:** Dobbs, B.J.T. (1975). *The Foundations of Newton's Alchemy: The Hunting of the Greene Lyon*. Cambridge University Press.
**Key finding:** Isaac Newton spent more time on alchemy than on physics; his alchemical notebooks reveal attempts to understand the active force binding matter at the micro-scale.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — hidden "Newton's Force" combination.
**Application:** A secret advanced combination (Newton's synthesis) unlocks a bonus level, referencing Dobbs's documentation that Newton sought a unifying principle behind material transformation.

### [Holmyard, 1957] — Alchemy
**Full citation:** Holmyard, E.J. (1957). *Alchemy*. Penguin Books.
**Key finding:** The concept of the philosopher's stone represents the perfecting agent — a catalyst that transforms base metals to gold and is itself unchanged; the concept spans Arabic, Latin, and Chinese alchemical traditions.
**Game mechanic:** Philosopher's Stone — ultimate alchemy mode unlock.
**Application:** Completing all four transmutation stages unlocks the Philosopher's Stone item, which temporarily auto-converts all DESPAIR tiles to PEACE tiles — the game's translation of alchemy's perfecting catalyst.

### [Multhauf, 1966] — The Origins of Chemistry
**Full citation:** Multhauf, R.P. (1966). *The Origins of Chemistry*. Oldbourne.
**Key finding:** The transition from alchemy to chemistry involved systematic quantification and replication; the key innovation was controlled experiment over mystical intuition.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — empirical vs. intuitive discovery paths.
**Application:** Players can discover combinations through intuitive (archetype-guided) paths or through systematic (empirical) testing in a "laboratory" sub-mode, reflecting Multhauf's historical transition from mysticism to method.

### [Needham, 1954] — Science and Civilisation in China (Vol. 5: Chemistry)
**Full citation:** Needham, J. (1954–1983). *Science and Civilisation in China, Vol. 5: Chemistry and Chemical Technology*. Cambridge University Press.
**Key finding:** Chinese alchemy (Neidan — internal alchemy) directed the same transformative principles inward; the body is a laboratory and the transformation is spiritual self-cultivation.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — inner vs. outer transmutation paths.
**Application:** Two parallel paths exist in Alchemy mode: Waidan (external — combining tiles in the grid) and Neidan (internal — combining breath + body scan + constellation patterns), based on Needham's documentation of Chinese dual-alchemy.

### [Eliade, 1978] — The Forge and the Crucible
**Full citation:** Eliade, M. (1978). *The Forge and the Crucible: The Origins and Structures of Alchemy*. University of Chicago Press.
**Key finding:** Smithcraft, metallurgy, and alchemy share a universal mythological structure: the transformation of raw ore into refined metal mirrors initiatory death-and-rebirth.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — forge ritual sequence.
**Application:** The Forge sub-mode within Alchemy presents a rhythmic tap sequence (hammer strikes) that maps the metalworking ritual Eliade describes, culminating in the "refined metal" transmutation result.

### [Atkins, 2010] — The Laws of Thermodynamics
**Full citation:** Atkins, P.W. (2010). *The Laws of Thermodynamics: A Very Short Introduction*. Oxford University Press.
**Key finding:** The Second Law — entropy always increases in closed systems — is irreversible; only energy input can temporarily create local order.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — entropy cost of transmutation.
**Application:** Every transmutation costs entropy (drawn from the player's PEACE reserves), making each transformation an energy-costly act that must be planned — reflecting Atkins's thermodynamic cost of creating local order.

### [Ball, 2006] — The Devil's Doctor: Paracelsus and the World of Renaissance Magic and Science
**Full citation:** Ball, P. (2006). *The Devil's Doctor: Paracelsus and the World of Renaissance Magic and Science*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
**Key finding:** Paracelsus insisted that the physician must know the natural world — minerals, plants, stars — as an interconnected system; healing requires understanding the whole.
**Game mechanic:** Cross-mode synthesis rewards.
**Application:** Players who combine discoveries across Alchemy, Ornithology, and Constellation modes unlock a Paracelsian "Whole System" bonus, reflecting Ball's documentation of Paracelsus's cross-domain philosophy.
