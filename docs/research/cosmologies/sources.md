# Cosmologies Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 13 — one per tradition)

### [Hindu — Bhagavad Gita, c.200 BCE] — The Song of the Lord
**Full citation:** *Bhagavad Gita* (c.200 BCE). Trans. Easwaran, E. (2007). *The Bhagavad Gita*. Nilgiri Press. (Original Sanskrit: public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/hin/gita/)
**Key finding:** The cosmos operates through the interplay of the three gunas — rajas (activity/passion), tamas (inertia/darkness), and sattva (clarity/harmony); liberation (*moksha*) comes through aligning action with dharma without attachment to outcome.
**Game mechanic:** Hindu cosmology mode — three-guna tile system.
**Application:** Tiles are classified by guna: RAGE/DESPAIR = tamas/rajas; PEACE = sattva. Achieving a sattva-dominant grid state without attachment (not collecting obsessively) rewards the non-attached action (*nishkama karma*) the Gita prescribes.

### [Buddhist — Pali Canon, c.3rd century BCE] — Dhammapada
**Full citation:** *Dhammapada* (c.3rd century BCE, Pali Canon). Trans. Buddharakkhita, A. (1985). *The Dhammapada*. Buddhist Publication Society. (Free text: https://www.accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/dhp/)
**Key finding:** The mind is the forerunner of all actions; suffering arises from craving and aversion; liberation comes through the Noble Eightfold Path — right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration.
**Game mechanic:** Buddhist cosmology mode — craving/aversion equilibrium.
**Application:** The Buddhist mode scores not on points collected but on equanimity — approaching PEACE tiles without craving and DESPAIR tiles without aversion. The player who maintains even-mindedness (*upekkhā*) across both scores highest.

### [Norse — Prose Edda, c.1220 CE] — Gylfaginning
**Full citation:** Snorri Sturluson. (c.1220 CE). *Prose Edda: Gylfaginning*. (Trans. Young, J.I. (1964). University of California Press. — Original: public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/pre/)
**Key finding:** Yggdrasil, the World Tree, connects nine realms; three wells at its roots supply wisdom (Mimir), fate (Urðr), and vitality; the worlds are in constant negotiation between order (Ásgarðr) and chaos (Jötunheimr).
**Game mechanic:** Norse cosmology mode — nine-realm world tree.
**Application:** The nine dreamscape biomes correspond to the nine Norse realms; Yggdrasil threads connect them visually. Completing a realm unlocks passage to adjacent realms according to the Eddic geography, not arbitrary level order.

### [Hermetic — Corpus Hermeticum, c.2nd–3rd century CE] — Poimandres
**Full citation:** *Corpus Hermeticum* (c.2nd–3rd century CE). Trans. Copenhaver, B.P. (1992). *Hermetica*. Cambridge University Press. (Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/chr/herm/)
**Key finding:** "As above, so below; as within, so without" — the macrocosm and microcosm mirror each other; the human being is a microcosmic image of the universe, capable of ascending through the planetary spheres through self-knowledge.
**Game mechanic:** Hermetic mode — macrocosm/microcosm correspondence.
**Application:** The planetary hour system in the game is a direct Hermetic implementation: each hour's planetary ruler corresponds to a macrocosmic quality that mirrors the player's inner state and the current game difficulty — as above, so below.

### [Taoist — Tao Te Ching, c.4th century BCE] — Chapters 1–81
**Full citation:** Laozi. (c.4th century BCE). *Tao Te Ching*. Trans. Le Guin, U.K. (1997). *Tao Te Ching: A Book About the Way and the Power of the Way*. Shambhala. (Original Chinese: public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/tao/taote.htm)
**Key finding:** The Tao (Way) is the unnameable source of all things; *wu wei* (non-striving, effortless action aligned with nature) is more effective than forceful effort; extremes return to their opposites (*fu*).
**Game mechanic:** Tao mode — wu wei scoring.
**Application:** In Tao mode, the highest score is achieved by collecting PEACE tiles effortlessly (without visible effort/rushing); the grid rewards patient, flowing movement over aggressive tile-hunting — embodying wu wei.

### [Stoic — Meditations, c.170 CE] — Marcus Aurelius
**Full citation:** Aurelius, M. (c.170 CE). *Meditations*. (Trans. Hays, G. (2002). Random House. — Original Greek: public domain. Free text: http://classics.mit.edu/Antoninus/meditations.html)
**Key finding:** The universe is a rational, providential whole (the Logos); every event is part of the cosmic order; the wise person accepts what cannot be changed while vigorously choosing virtue within their control.
**Game mechanic:** Stoic mode — acceptance + agency balance scoring.
**Application:** Stoic mode presents uncontrollable adverse tile events (weather, random DESPAIR bursts); the player scores by accepting those without health penalty (non-resistance) while actively choosing virtuous responses in their area of control.

### [Egyptian — Book of the Dead, c.1550 BCE] — The Papyrus of Ani
**Full citation:** *Book of the Dead* (c.1550 BCE). Trans. Budge, E.A.W. (1895). *The Book of the Dead: The Papyrus of Ani*. British Museum. (Public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/egy/ebod/)
**Key finding:** The soul (*ba*) undergoes 42 negative confessions before Osiris's judgment; each declares freedom from a specific harmful action — the heart is weighed against Ma'at's feather of truth.
**Game mechanic:** Egyptian cosmology mode — Ma'at judgment trial.
**Application:** At the end of each Egyptian dreamscape session, the player's tile choices are weighed against 42 criteria (honesty, compassion, non-harm); a heart lighter than the feather unlocks the Field of Reeds (bonus level) — a direct game translation of the Papyrus of Ani.

### [Mayan — Popol Vuh, c.1550 CE transcription] — Creation Narrative
**Full citation:** *Popol Vuh* (transcribed c.1550 CE from oral tradition). Trans. Tedlock, D. (1985). *Popol Vuh: The Definitive Edition of the Mayan Book of the Dawn of Life*. Simon & Schuster. (Original text: public domain.)
**Key finding:** The Hero Twins Hunahpu and Xbalanque descend to Xibalba (the underworld), defeat the Lords of Death through wit and skill, and ascend to become the sun and moon — death and resurrection as cosmic creative act.
**Game mechanic:** Underworld mode — descent and return arc.
**Application:** The Underworld mode follows the Popol Vuh structure: descent (grid darkens, DESPAIR tiles multiply), trials (skill challenges), and ascent (PEACE restoration) — the Hero Twins' journey as a complete recovery arc narrative.

### [I Ching, c.1000 BCE] — Book of Changes
**Full citation:** *I Ching* (c.1000 BCE). Trans. Wilhelm, R., & Baynes, C.F. (1950). *The I Ching or Book of Changes*. Princeton University Press. (Original Chinese: public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/ich/)
**Key finding:** 64 hexagrams describe every possible situation arising from the interplay of yin and yang; change is constant and the sage aligns with the direction of change rather than resisting it.
**Game mechanic:** Daily challenge hexagram generator.
**Application:** Each day's challenge is determined by a pseudo-random hexagram draw (64 possible configurations); the hexagram's traditional description provides the day's thematic frame, and the challenge's tile-configuration matches the hexagram's yin-yang balance.

### [Celtic — Mabinogion, c.11th–13th century CE] — The Four Branches
**Full citation:** *Mabinogion* (c.11th–13th century CE). Trans. Guest, C. (1838–1849). (Public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/neu/mab/)
**Key finding:** The Otherworld (*Annwn*) interpenetrates the ordinary world; sovereignty goddesses and shape-shifting beings test human virtue through unexpected trials; the hero who maintains integrity through transformation wins sovereignty.
**Game mechanic:** Celtic cosmology mode — Otherworld overlay.
**Application:** In Celtic mode, a second "Otherworld" grid overlaps the normal grid; tiles flicker between worlds. Players must identify which tier a tile truly belongs to (normal or Otherworld) — testing discernment, the virtue the Mabinogion's trials reward.

### [Confucian — Analects, c.475 BCE] — Lunyu
**Full citation:** Confucius. (c.475 BCE). *Lunyu* [The Analects]. Trans. Legge, J. (1893). Clarendon Press. (Public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/cfu/conf1.htm)
**Key finding:** Moral cultivation (*ren* — humaneness) is achieved through daily self-examination and ritual practice; the superior person (*junzi*) continuously refines their conduct through reflection on the day's actions.
**Game mechanic:** Daily session debrief — Confucian self-examination.
**Application:** The end-of-session debrief asks three Confucian questions: "Was I faithful in tile choices? Was I sincere in my recovery commitment? Did I practice skills I have not yet mastered?" Honest answers unlock a daily cultivation bonus.

### [Gnostic — Gospel of Thomas, c.50–100 CE] — Sayings of Jesus
**Full citation:** *Gospel of Thomas* (c.50–100 CE). Trans. Meyer, M. (1992). In *The Gnostic Gospels*. HarperCollins. (Coptic text: public domain. Free text: https://www.sacred-texts.com/chr/thomas.htm)
**Key finding:** "The Kingdom of Heaven is spread upon the earth, and men do not see it" — illumination is not a distant attainment but a perceptual shift available in the present moment to those who seek within.
**Game mechanic:** Hidden tile system — revelation mechanic.
**Application:** A set of PEACE tiles is hidden in plain sight in every grid (visually indistinct from background); the Gnostic mode trains players to perceive what is already there. Discovering hidden tiles triggers the "gnosis" effect — "You have found what was always here."

### [Indigenous Australian — Songlines, c.50,000+ BCE oral tradition] — Dreaming
**Full citation:** Chatwin, B. (1987). *The Songlines*. Viking. (Ethnographic account.) See also: Munn, N.D. (1973). *Warlpiri Iconography*. Cornell University Press.
**Key finding:** The Songlines are navigational paths across Australia encoded in ancestral song; landscape features are simultaneously physical geography and sacred narrative; walking the song is the act of creation.
**Game mechanic:** Dreamscape path navigation — songline walking.
**Application:** The dreamscape map contains hidden Songline paths; walking specific tile sequences in the correct order (following the song rather than the shortest path) reveals sacred sites and bonus content — the journey itself is the reward, not the destination.
