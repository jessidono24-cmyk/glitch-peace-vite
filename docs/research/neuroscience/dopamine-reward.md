# Dopamine & Reward Systems

## Sources
- Schultz, W. (1997). "A neural substrate of prediction and reward." *Science*, 275(5306), 1593-1599.
- Schultz, W. et al. (1997). "A neural substrate of prediction and reward." *Science*, 275, 1593-1599.
- Berridge, K.C. & Kringelbach, M.L. (2015). "Pleasure systems in the brain." *Neuron*, 86(3), 646-664.
- Bromberg-Martin, E.S. et al. (2010). "Dopamine in motivational control: Rewarding, aversive, and alerting." *Neuron*, 68(5), 815-834.

## Key Findings
1. Dopamine neurons fire to **unexpected rewards** and **reward-predicting cues**, not rewards themselves after learning
2. **Reward Prediction Error (RPE)**: Dopamine signal = actual reward − expected reward
   - Better-than-expected → positive RPE → dopamine burst → approach behavior reinforced
   - Worse-than-expected → negative RPE → dopamine dip → avoidance behavior reinforced
   - As-expected → no RPE → no dopamine response (habituation)
3. Variable ratio reinforcement schedules produce highest response rates and strongest habit formation
4. Dopamine is fundamentally about **motivation and wanting**, not hedonic pleasure per se
5. Social rewards and abstract rewards activate the same dopaminergic circuits as primary rewards

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- PEACE tile collection should implement RPE mechanics: predictable patterns give less reward than surprise clusters
- Variable reward schedule: occasional "bonus PEACE burst" at unexpected moments maximizes engagement
- Progress feedback should front-load surprise (early game feels magical) then sustain mastery satisfaction
- Social/achievement rewards (archetype unlocks, emergence level ups) activate same circuits
