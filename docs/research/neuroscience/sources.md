# Neuroscience Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 10)

### [Berridge & Robinson, 1998] — What Is the Role of Dopamine in Reward?
**Full citation:** Berridge, K.C., & Robinson, T.E. (1998). What is the role of dopamine in reward: Hedonic impact, reward learning, or incentive salience? *Brain Research Reviews*, 28(3), 309–369.
**Key finding:** Dopamine mediates *wanting* (incentive salience) independently of *liking* (hedonic impact); addiction involves escalating wanting without increased liking.
**Game mechanic:** Craving cycle simulation — DESPAIR/RAGE tile pull.
**Application:** DESPAIR tiles attract the player (wanting signal) while offering no positive outcome (liking absent), simulating the dopaminergic wanting-without-liking cycle of addiction.

### [Robinson & Berridge, 1993] — Neural Basis of Drug Craving
**Full citation:** Robinson, T.E., & Berridge, K.C. (1993). The neural basis of drug craving: An incentive-salience theory of addiction. *Brain Research Reviews*, 18(3), 247–291.
**Key finding:** Repeated stimulation sensitizes incentive-salience pathways, causing compulsive approach behavior even when the object is no longer enjoyable.
**Game mechanic:** Incentive salience model for craving tiles.
**Application:** RAGE/DESPAIR tiles are designed with high visual salience (size, color pulse) to activate incentive-salience circuits, making them deliberately tempting to study impulse control.

### [Koob & Volkow, 2010] — Neurobiology of Addiction
**Full citation:** Koob, G.F., & Volkow, N.D. (2010). Neurocircuitry of addiction. *Neuropsychopharmacology*, 35(1), 217–238.
**Key finding:** Addiction involves three stages — binge/intoxication, withdrawal/negative affect, preoccupation/anticipation — each recruiting distinct brain circuits.
**Game mechanic:** Health bar three-phase design (full, depleted, crisis).
**Application:** The health bar's three visual states correspond to the Koob-Volkow three-stage model; UI feedback changes at each threshold to mirror the neurobiological shift.

### [Hebb, 1949] — The Organization of Behavior
**Full citation:** Hebb, D.O. (1949). *The Organization of Behavior*. Wiley.
**Key finding:** "Neurons that fire together wire together" — co-activation of neurons strengthens synaptic connections, the cellular basis of learning.
**Game mechanic:** Pattern recognition training — repeated PEACE tile chains.
**Application:** Each PEACE tile chain repetition co-activates calm-state circuits, using Hebbian plasticity to rewire automatic responses toward peace rather than reactivity.

### [Doidge, 2007] — The Brain That Changes Itself
**Full citation:** Doidge, N. (2007). *The Brain That Changes Itself*. Viking.
**Key finding:** Neuroplasticity is activity-dependent and lifelong; focused repetitive practice restructures cortical maps.
**Game mechanic:** Emergence level progression — cortical reorganization milestone markers.
**Application:** Each emergence level is calibrated to reflect a measurable increase in automaticity (reaction time + accuracy), treating level advancement as a proxy for cortical map reorganization.

### [Porges, 2011] — The Polyvagal Theory
**Full citation:** Porges, S.W. (2011). *The Polyvagal Theory: Neurophysiological Foundations of Emotions, Attachment, Communication, and Self-regulation*. Norton.
**Key finding:** The ventral vagal complex supports social engagement and calm; activation through slow breathing inhibits fight-or-flight responses.
**Game mechanic:** Breath prompt system (4-7-8 breathing).
**Application:** The 4-7-8 breath pattern activates vagal tone via slow exhalation, shifting autonomic state from sympathetic arousal to ventral vagal calm before difficult game sections.

### [Craig, 2002] — How Do You Feel?
**Full citation:** Craig, A.D. (2002). How do you feel? Interoception: The sense of the physiological condition of the body. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 3(8), 655–666.
**Key finding:** The anterior insular cortex integrates interoceptive signals, creating the subjective experience of bodily feelings that underlie all emotions.
**Game mechanic:** Embodiment score — interoceptive accuracy tracking.
**Application:** Body scan tile sequences train the player's attention toward internal bodily signals, exercising the interoceptive pathway that Craig identifies as the substrate of emotional awareness.

### [Levin, 2021] — Bioelectric Signaling
**Full citation:** Levin, M. (2021). Bioelectric signaling: Reprogrammable circuits underlying embryogenesis, regeneration, and cancer. *Cell*, 184(8), 1971–1989.
**Key finding:** Bioelectric fields encode pattern memory in tissues independently of DNA; voltage gradients guide growth and repair.
**Game mechanic:** Reaction-diffusion connections in Constellation mode.
**Application:** Constellation lines between stars pulse with a reaction-diffusion animation inspired by Turing patterning, visualizing the bioelectric communication model Levin describes.

### [Dehaene, 2014] — Consciousness and the Brain
**Full citation:** Dehaene, S. (2014). *Consciousness and the Brain: Deciphering How the Brain Codes Our Thoughts*. Viking.
**Key finding:** Consciousness arises from a "global ignition" — a phase transition in which a neural representation suddenly becomes globally broadcast across the cortex.
**Game mechanic:** Phase transition activation in Constellation mode.
**Application:** When a player completes a constellation pattern, the visual "ignition" animation mirrors Dehaene's global workspace ignition: a sudden flash of light propagating outward from the pattern.

### [Sapolsky, 2004] — Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers
**Full citation:** Sapolsky, R.M. (2004). *Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers* (3rd ed.). Holt Paperbacks.
**Key finding:** Chronic psychological stress (unlike acute stress) maintains elevated glucocorticoids, damaging hippocampal neurons and impairing memory and emotional regulation.
**Game mechanic:** RAGE tile health drain — glucocorticoid stress response modeling.
**Application:** The RAGE tile's health drain accelerates when tiles are collected rapidly without pause, mirroring the neurological damage of chronic unrelieved stress vs. acute stress.

### [Kabat-Zinn, 1990] — Full Catastrophe Living
**Full citation:** Kabat-Zinn, J. (1990). *Full Catastrophe Living: Using the Wisdom of Your Body and Mind to Face Stress, Pain, and Illness*. Delacorte Press.
**Key finding:** Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) produces measurable reductions in self-reported stress, anxiety, and chronic pain through sustained non-judgmental attention.
**Game mechanic:** Body scan tiles in Meditation mode.
**Application:** The body scan tile sequence follows Kabat-Zinn's MBSR protocol: systematic attention moving from feet to head, each tile prompting awareness of a body region.

### [Siegel, 2012] — The Developing Mind
**Full citation:** Siegel, D.J. (2012). *The Developing Mind: How Relationships and the Brain Interact to Shape Who We Are* (2nd ed.). Guilford Press.
**Key finding:** Interpersonal neurobiology shows that relationship patterns are encoded in neural circuits; secure relationships literally reshape the brain toward regulation.
**Game mechanic:** RPG relational choices.
**Application:** Relational choices in the RPG mode activate different attachment scripts (secure, avoidant, anxious), allowing players to practice secure-attachment responses and strengthen those neural circuits.
