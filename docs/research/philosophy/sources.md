# Philosophy Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 10)

### [Aurelius, c.170 CE] — Meditations
**Full citation:** Aurelius, M. (c.170 CE). *Meditations*. (Trans. Hays, G. (2002). *Meditations: A New Translation*. Modern Library. — Original text: public domain, various translations.)
**Key finding:** The Stoic discipline of assent: between stimulus and response there is a gap; one chooses whether to assent to an impression and act on it — freedom lies in this gap.
**Game mechanic:** Impulse buffer delay — Stoic gap mechanic.
**Application:** The impulse buffer delay before acting on RAGE/DESPAIR tiles is explicitly framed as the Stoic "gap" — a visual countdown represents the moment of non-assent, making Aurelius's philosophical practice a physical gameplay action.

### [Epictetus, c.108 CE] — Discourses
**Full citation:** Epictetus. (c.108 CE). *Discourses*. (Trans. Oldfather, W.A. (1925). Loeb Classical Library. — Public domain.)
**Key finding:** The Dichotomy of Control: some things are "up to us" (opinions, impulses, desires, aversions) and some are not; wisdom consists in focusing only on what is up to us.
**Game mechanic:** Recovery mode — locus of control framing.
**Application:** The game UI consistently marks which health parameters are "in your control" (tile choices, breathing, session length) vs. "not in your control" (random tile spawn), following Epictetus's Dichotomy in a literal game-design frame.

### [James, 1890] — The Principles of Psychology
**Full citation:** James, W. (1890). *The Principles of Psychology* (2 vols.). Henry Holt.
**Key finding:** Habit is the flywheel of society; habits are formed through repetition and become automatic, below the threshold of conscious attention, freeing attention for higher activity.
**Game mechanic:** Habit formation loop — daily play streak.
**Application:** The daily streak system is designed to build habit automaticity (James's flywheel); after 21 days, the streak UI shifts from explicit reminder to background notification, reflecting the reduced conscious effort of an established habit.

### [Merleau-Ponty, 1945] — Phenomenology of Perception
**Full citation:** Merleau-Ponty, M. (1945). *Phénoménologie de la Perception* [Phenomenology of Perception]. Gallimard. (Trans. Smith, C. (1962). Routledge.)
**Key finding:** Perception is always embodied — not a disembodied mind receiving data, but a living body in skilled engagement with its environment; the body is the subject of perception, not its object.
**Game mechanic:** Embodiment score — body-as-subject mechanic.
**Application:** The embodiment score increases when players attend to body sensations during gameplay (interoceptive focus mode); it decreases when playing purely cognitively without body awareness, operationalizing Merleau-Ponty's embodied-subject principle.

### [Kierkegaard, 1843] — Either/Or
**Full citation:** Kierkegaard, S. (1843). *Enten-Eller* [Either/Or]. (Trans. Hong, H.V., & Hong, E.H. (1987). Princeton University Press.)
**Key finding:** Human existence involves a choice between the aesthetic stage (immediate sensory pleasure), the ethical stage (duty/commitment), and the religious stage (relationship to the absolute); the aesthetic stage leads to despair when its satisfactions are exhausted.
**Game mechanic:** Alchemy mode — three-stage existence model.
**Application:** Alchemy mode's three transmutation paths correspond to Kierkegaard's three existence stages: Sensory (aesthetic), Commitment (ethical), and Transcendence (religious) — the player's chosen path determines which transmutation outcomes are available.

### [Husserl, 1913] — Ideas I
**Full citation:** Husserl, E. (1913). *Ideen zu einer reinen Phänomenologie* [Ideas I]. Max Niemeyer. (Trans. Kersten, F. (1983). Martinus Nijhoff.)
**Key finding:** The phenomenological reduction (*epoché*) — bracketing assumptions about external reality — reveals the structure of pure conscious experience; intentionality (consciousness is always of something) is its fundamental structure.
**Game mechanic:** Meditation mode — phenomenological attention training.
**Application:** The meditation mode's attention exercises explicitly train epoché: players bracket the "tile narrative" and attend purely to the geometrical qualities of shape, color, and motion — practicing the reduction Husserl describes.

### [Whitehead, 1929] — Process and Reality
**Full citation:** Whitehead, A.N. (1929). *Process and Reality: An Essay in Cosmology*. Macmillan.
**Key finding:** Reality is constituted by processes ("actual occasions") rather than static substances; every actual occasion prehends (takes account of) all prior occasions — experience is relational and cumulative.
**Game mechanic:** Constellation mode — accumulation and prehension.
**Application:** Constellation nodes accumulate the "energy" of all previous correct identifications by the player (prehension); a node's brightness reflects its relational history, not just its intrinsic value — modeling Whitehead's process ontology.

### [Nagel, 1974] — What Is It Like to Be a Bat?
**Full citation:** Nagel, T. (1974). What is it like to be a bat? *Philosophical Review*, 83(4), 435–450.
**Key finding:** Subjective experience (qualia) has an irreducible first-person character that third-person objective description cannot capture; consciousness involves an irreducibly phenomenal "what it's like."
**Game mechanic:** Ornithology mode — species-perspective shifts.
**Application:** The Ornithology mode includes first-person bird perspective encounters, where the visual field, sound palette, and navigation logic shift to match the target species' sensory world — operationalizing Nagel's question as a playable experience.

### [Camus, 1942] — The Myth of Sisyphus
**Full citation:** Camus, A. (1942). *Le Mythe de Sisyphe* [The Myth of Sisyphus]. Gallimard. (Trans. O'Brien, J. (1955). Knopf.)
**Key finding:** The Absurd arises from the conflict between human longing for meaning and the universe's silence; the authentic response is rebellious engagement with life despite the absurdity.
**Game mechanic:** Recovery mode — the Sisyphus interpretation of relapse.
**Application:** When a player relapses (health drops to zero), the screen shows Sisyphus pushing the boulder back up — not as failure, but as return. Text reads: "One must imagine Sisyphus happy." The next session begins without penalty, normalizing the relapse-recovery cycle.

### [Wittgenstein, 1953] — Philosophical Investigations
**Full citation:** Wittgenstein, L. (1953). *Philosophische Untersuchungen* [Philosophical Investigations]. Blackwell. (Trans. Anscombe, G.E.M.)
**Key finding:** Meaning is use — words mean what they do in practiced language games; private language is impossible because meaning requires public, shared criteria.
**Game mechanic:** Language Learning mode — use-in-context vocabulary scoring.
**Application:** Words in Language Learning mode are scored only when used correctly in a communicative context (not on recall tests alone), applying Wittgenstein's use-theory of meaning: a word learned in isolation but never used "in a language game" earns only partial credit.
