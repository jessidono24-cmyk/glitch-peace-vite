# Emotional Regulation Research

## Sources
- Gross, J.J. (1998). "The emerging field of emotion regulation: An integrative review." *Review of General Psychology*, 2(3), 271-299.
- Plutchik, R. (1980). *Emotion: A Psychoevolutionary Synthesis*. Harper & Row.
- Linehan, M.M. (1993). *Cognitive-Behavioral Treatment of Borderline Personality Disorder*. Guilford.
- Kabat-Zinn, J. (1994). *Wherever You Go, There You Are: Mindfulness Meditation in Everyday Life*. Hyperion.

## Key Findings
1. **Plutchik's wheel**: 8 primary emotions (joy, trust, fear, surprise, sadness, disgust, anger, anticipation) combine into complex secondary emotions
2. Emotion regulation strategies: Cognitive reappraisal (most effective long-term); suppression (effective short-term, costly long-term)
3. DBT skills (Linehan): Distress tolerance, emotion regulation, mindfulness, interpersonal effectiveness
4. Mindfulness reduces amygdala reactivity and increases PFC regulation capacity
5. Emotional granularity (precise labeling of emotional states) reduces emotional intensity

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- Emotional field color system maps to Plutchik's wheel
- Tile types correspond to emotional states on the wheel (PEACE = joy/trust, DESPAIR = sadness, RAGE = anger)
- Health bar as emotional regulation metaphor: drain = dysregulation, regeneration = return to baseline
- Meditation mode teaches distress tolerance (sitting with discomfort without acting out)
