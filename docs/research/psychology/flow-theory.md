# Flow Theory

## Sources
- Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990). *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience*. Harper & Row.
- Nakamura, J. & Csikszentmihalyi, M. (2002). "The concept of flow." In C.R. Snyder & S.J. Lopez (Eds.), *Handbook of Positive Psychology* (pp. 89-105). Oxford University Press.
- Kotler, S. (2014). *The Rise of Superman: Decoding the Science of Ultimate Human Performance*. New Harvest.

## Key Findings
1. Flow occurs when **challenge and skill are balanced** — challenge slightly above current skill level
2. Nine dimensions: Clear goals; unambiguous feedback; challenge-skill balance; action-awareness merging; concentration; sense of control; loss of self-consciousness; time distortion; autotelic experience
3. Flow requires **intrinsic motivation** — the activity must be valued for itself
4. Neurologically: flow correlates with transient hypofrontality (reduced PFC self-monitoring activity)
5. Peak performance and peak experience coincide in flow
