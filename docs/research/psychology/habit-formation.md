# Habit Formation Research

## Sources
- Duhigg, C. (2012). *The Power of Habit: Why We Do What We Do in Life and Business*. Random House.
- Wood, W. & Neal, D.T. (2007). "A new look at habits and the habit-goal interface." *Psychological Review*, 114(4), 843-863.
- Lally, P. et al. (2010). "How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world." *European Journal of Social Psychology*, 40(6), 998-1009.
- Verplanken, B. & Wood, W. (2006). "Interventions to break and create consumer habits." *Journal of Public Policy & Marketing*, 25(1), 90-103.

## Key Findings
1. **Habit loop**: Cue → Routine → Reward; habits encoded in basal ganglia, not cortex
2. Average habit formation time: **66 days** (range: 18–254 days; Lally et al., 2010)
3. Missing one day doesn't significantly disrupt habit formation — consistency over time matters
4. **Implementation intentions** ("When X happens, I will do Y") dramatically increase habit formation success
5. Environment design is more powerful than willpower for sustaining habits

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- Daily session design creates habit loop: App open (cue) → play session (routine) → completion reward (reward)
- Streak tracking encodes habit identity ("I am someone who practices daily")
- Implementation intentions: "After I wake up, I will play one session" supported by daily unlock design
- Environment design: Mode selection screen as commitment architecture
