# Implicit Learning Theory

## Sources
- Reber, A.S. (1967). "Implicit learning of artificial grammars." *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior*, 6(6), 855-863.
- Reber, A.S. (1993). *Implicit Learning and Tacit Knowledge: An Essay on the Cognitive Unconscious*. Oxford University Press.
- Cleeremans, A. & McClelland, J.L. (1991). "Learning the structure of event sequences." *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*, 120(3), 235-253.
- Squire, L.R. (2004). "Memory systems of the brain: A brief history and current perspective." *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory*, 82(3), 171-177.

## Key Findings
1. Implicit learning occurs automatically during exposure, without intentional effort to learn
2. Learners extract statistical regularities from complex structured input without being able to articulate the rules
3. Implicit knowledge is more **robust and durable** than explicit knowledge under stress/distraction
4. Implicit learning is relatively unaffected by age, IQ, and working memory capacity
5. Optimal conditions: rich, complex input; extended exposure; no pressure to perform

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- Grid tile patterns teach statistical regularities (which sequences lead to PEACE vs. DESPAIR)
- Players learn enemy AI patterns before they can describe them
- Emotional field color associations become automatic before conscious awareness
- Network topology (mycology, constellation modes) acquired through play, not instruction
