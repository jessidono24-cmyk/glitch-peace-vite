# Relapse Prevention Research

## Sources
- Marlatt, G.A. & Gordon, J.R. (1985). *Relapse Prevention: Maintenance Strategies in the Treatment of Addictive Behaviors*. Guilford.
- Witkiewitz, K. & Marlatt, G.A. (2004). "Relapse prevention for alcohol and drug problems." *American Psychologist*, 59(4), 224-235.
- Bowen, S. et al. (2014). "Relative efficacy of mindfulness-based relapse prevention, standard relapse prevention, and treatment as usual for substance use disorders." *JAMA Psychiatry*, 71(5), 547-556.

## Key Findings
1. **High-risk situations**: Negative emotional states (35%), interpersonal conflict (16%), social pressure (20%) — account for 71% of relapses
2. **Abstinence violation effect (AVE)**: First lapse often leads to full relapse due to all-or-nothing thinking; cognitive reframing prevents this
3. **Mindfulness-Based Relapse Prevention (MBRP)**: Significantly better long-term outcomes than standard RP
4. **Urge surfing**: Observing craving as a wave (rises, peaks, falls) without acting on it reduces craving duration and intensity
5. Relapse is part of recovery — not failure; learning from lapses builds resilience

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- Pattern training (PEACE collection) directly rehearses alternative behavioral responses to craving cues
- RAGE/DESPAIR tile encounters as high-risk situation simulation — player practices navigating without acting out
- Health loss from lapse (entering DESPAIR zone) is recoverable — counters AVE/all-or-nothing thinking
- Consequence preview mechanic (`src/recovery/consequence-preview.js`) models future-self visualization
- "Urge surfing" mode: extended DESPAIR exposure with gradual health recovery if player remains still
