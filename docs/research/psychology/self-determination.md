# Self-Determination Theory

## Sources
- Deci, E.L. & Ryan, R.M. (1985). *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*. Plenum.
- Ryan, R.M. & Deci, E.L. (2000). "Self-determination theory and the facilitation of intrinsic motivation, social development, and well-being." *American Psychologist*, 55(1), 68-78.
- Przybylski, A.K. et al. (2010). "Motivational, emotional, and behavioral correlates of fear of missing out." *Computers in Human Behavior*, 29(4), 1841-1848.

## Key Findings
1. **Autonomy**: People need to feel their actions are self-chosen (not coerced)
2. **Competence**: People need to feel effective and capable
3. **Relatedness**: People need to feel connected to others
4. Controlling rewards (contingent on performance) undermine intrinsic motivation
5. Informational feedback (non-controlling) enhances intrinsic motivation

## Application in GLITCH·PEACE
- **Autonomy**: Mode selection, archetype choice, play style freedom
- **Competence**: Emergence level progression, visible skill improvement, mastery rewards
- **Relatedness**: Co-op mode, community features, narrative connection to archetypes
- Avoid punitive scoring that undermines autonomy; emphasize growth over performance
