# Psychology Research Sources

## Sources (minimum 10)

### [Csikszentmihalyi, 1990] — Flow: Psychology of Optimal Experience
**Full citation:** Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1990). *Flow: The Psychology of Optimal Experience*. Harper & Row.
**Key finding:** Flow occurs when perceived challenge and skill are in balance, producing deep concentration, loss of self-consciousness, and intrinsic reward.
**Game mechanic:** Dynamic Difficulty Adjustment (DDA) — flow channel targeting.
**Application:** The DDA system continuously measures player performance and adjusts tile spawn rates to keep challenge just above current skill, sustaining the flow state.

### [Reber, 1967] — Implicit Learning of Artificial Grammars
**Full citation:** Reber, A.S. (1967). Implicit learning of artificial grammars. *Journal of Verbal Learning and Verbal Behavior*, 6(6), 855–863.
**Key finding:** People acquire complex rule systems through passive exposure without conscious awareness of the rules being learned.
**Game mechanic:** Pattern recognition training — tile identification improving over sessions.
**Application:** The grid's repeating emotional-tile patterns are absorbed implicitly; players recognize PEACE/DESPAIR clusters without ever being told the rules.

### [Deci & Ryan, 1985] — Self-Determination Theory
**Full citation:** Deci, E.L., & Ryan, R.M. (1985). *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*. Plenum Press.
**Key finding:** Autonomous motivation (intrinsic goals) produces more durable behavior change than controlled motivation (external rewards).
**Game mechanic:** Archetype power system — identity-level motivation.
**Application:** Players choose an archetype that aligns with personal values, shifting motivation from "earn points" to "become who I choose to be."

### [Gross, 2001] — Emotion Regulation in Adulthood
**Full citation:** Gross, J.J. (2001). Emotion regulation in adulthood: Timing is everything. *Current Directions in Psychological Science*, 10(6), 214–219.
**Key finding:** Antecedent-focused strategies (reappraisal) are more effective and less cognitively costly than response-focused strategies (suppression).
**Game mechanic:** Emotional field color system — reappraisal training through gameplay.
**Application:** Navigating tiles of different emotional valences trains the player to reappraise emotional content rather than suppress, mirroring cognitive reappraisal strategies.

### [Fitts & Posner, 1967] — Human Performance
**Full citation:** Fitts, P.M., & Posner, M.I. (1967). *Human Performance*. Wadsworth.
**Key finding:** Skill acquisition progresses through three stages: cognitive (effortful, rule-following), associative (refining), and autonomous (automatic, effortless).
**Game mechanic:** Emergence levels 1–5.
**Application:** The five emergence levels correspond to milestones in the Fitts–Posner model; reaching level 5 signals autonomous mastery of the game's emotional navigation rules.

### [Marlatt & Gordon, 1985] — Relapse Prevention
**Full citation:** Marlatt, G.A., & Gordon, J.R. (Eds.) (1985). *Relapse Prevention: Maintenance Strategies in the Treatment of Addictive Behaviors*. Guilford Press.
**Key finding:** Urge surfing — observing cravings without acting on them — reduces the reinforcing value of addictive stimuli over time.
**Game mechanic:** Impulse buffer delay before RAGE/DESPAIR tile collection.
**Application:** A timed delay before the player can collect high-risk tiles mirrors urge-surfing, training the pause-and-observe skill critical to relapse prevention.

### [Kahneman, 2011] — Thinking, Fast and Slow
**Full citation:** Kahneman, D. (2011). *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. Farrar, Straus and Giroux.
**Key finding:** Human cognition operates via a fast, automatic System 1 and a slow, deliberate System 2; most errors occur when System 1 is triggered inappropriately.
**Game mechanic:** Matrix A/B toggle — switching between reactive and deliberate modes.
**Application:** Matrix A is System 1 (fast, pattern-based); Matrix B is System 2 (slower, analytical). The toggle explicitly trains metacognitive awareness of which mode is active.

### [Bandura, 1977] — Self-Efficacy Theory
**Full citation:** Bandura, A. (1977). Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change. *Psychological Review*, 84(2), 191–215.
**Key finding:** Perceived self-efficacy — belief in one's ability to execute required behavior — is the strongest predictor of behavior initiation and persistence.
**Game mechanic:** Emergence level progression feedback.
**Application:** Each emergence level milestone provides mastery experience (the strongest source of self-efficacy), building player's belief in their own recovery capacity.

### [Hershfield et al., 2011] — Increasing Saving Behavior Through Age-Progressed Renderings
**Full citation:** Hershfield, H.E., Goldstein, D.G., Sharpe, W.F., Fox, J., Yeykelis, L., Carstensen, L.L., & Bailenson, J.N. (2011). Increasing saving behavior through age-progressed renderings of the future self. *Journal of Marketing Research*, 48, S23–S37.
**Key finding:** Vividly experiencing the future self increases present-day decisions aligned with long-term wellbeing.
**Game mechanic:** Consequence preview mechanic — future-self visualization before risky tile collection.
**Application:** A brief animation showing downstream health consequences (future self in a depleted state) before collecting RAGE tiles leverages future-self vividness to discourage impulsive collection.

### [Russell, 1980] — A Circumplex Model of Affect
**Full citation:** Russell, J.A. (1980). A circumplex model of affect. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 39(6), 1161–1178.
**Key finding:** All emotional states can be mapped on two dimensions: valence (pleasant–unpleasant) and arousal (activated–deactivated).
**Game mechanic:** 17 tile types — full emotional taxonomy.
**Application:** The 17 tile types are positioned across the Russell circumplex, ensuring full coverage of emotional space so every gameplay encounter involves a different position on the valence-arousal plane.

### [Bouton, 2002] — Context, Ambiguity, and Unlearning
**Full citation:** Bouton, M.E. (2002). Context, ambiguity, and unlearning: Sources of relapse after behavioral extinction. *Biological Psychiatry*, 52(10), 976–986.
**Key finding:** Extinction does not erase prior conditioning; new learning overlays old associations and is context-dependent, explaining why relapse is context-triggered.
**Game mechanic:** PEACE pattern training — reconditioning through repeated peaceful-tile collection.
**Application:** The game trains PEACE-association responses that overlay (not delete) prior stress-responses, which is why the game recommends daily play to maintain the new context of calm association.

### [Plutchik, 1980] — Emotion: A Psychoevolutionary Synthesis
**Full citation:** Plutchik, R. (1980). *Emotion: A Psychoevolutionary Synthesis*. Harper & Row.
**Key finding:** There are eight basic emotions arranged in opposing pairs; all complex emotions are blends of these primaries.
**Game mechanic:** Emotional field color coding.
**Application:** The emotional field uses Plutchik's color wheel — each primary emotion is assigned a hue, and mixed-emotion tiles use blended colors, providing visual intuition about emotional complexity.
