# Addiction Recovery Model — Synthesis

## Overview
This document synthesizes neuroscience, psychology, somatic, and wisdom-tradition perspectives on addiction recovery into a coherent model informing GLITCH·PEACE's design as a recovery tool.

## The Multi-Level Model of Addiction

### Level 1: Neurobiological (Circuits)
*See `docs/research/neuroscience/addiction-circuits.md` for full detail*

- Dopamine reward prediction error drives behavioral reinforcement
- PFC dysregulation enables compulsive responding despite negative consequences
- Stress systems (CRF, cortisol) lower threshold for relapse
- **Recovery target**: Restore dopamine receptor density; rebuild PFC regulation; retrain stress response

### Level 2: Psychological (Patterns)
*See `docs/research/psychology/relapse-prevention.md` for full detail*

- Addictive behavior develops from repeated incentive-salience hijacking
- Cognitive distortions (all-or-nothing, catastrophizing) maintain the cycle
- Emotional avoidance drives substance use (short-term relief, long-term dysregulation)
- **Recovery target**: Build distress tolerance; develop cognitive flexibility; establish new reward patterns

### Level 3: Somatic (Body)
*See `docs/research/neuroscience/somatic-fascia.md` for full detail*

- Trauma is stored in body as chronic fascial tension and autonomic dysregulation
- Physiological state directly drives craving intensity (interoceptive feedback loop)
- **Recovery target**: Restore ventral vagal regulation; build interoceptive awareness; release fascial holdings

### Level 4: Behavioral (Habits)
*See `docs/research/psychology/habit-formation.md` for full detail*

- Addictive behaviors are deeply encoded habit loops (cue-routine-reward)
- New habit formation requires 66+ days of consistent alternative behavior
- **Recovery target**: Install competing habit loops; redesign environment to reduce cue exposure

### Level 5: Identity (Narrative)
- Addiction is maintained partly by identity ("I am an addict/user")
- Recovery requires identity transformation ("I am someone in recovery/someone who practices peace")
- Narrative medicine: constructing a coherent recovery story supports sustainable change

### Level 6: Spiritual/Meaning (Existential)
- 12-step programs emphasize spiritual dimension for a reason: meaning deficits drive substance use
- Viktor Frankl's logotherapy: "Will to meaning" — finding purpose is protective against addiction
- **Recovery target**: Connect actions to larger meaning and values

## Evidence Summary
- Pattern training as addiction recovery: supported by CBT evidence base (Beck, 2011)
- Game-based interventions for addiction: emerging evidence (Ceranoglu, 2010; Kato, 2010)
- Mindfulness-based relapse prevention: Level 1 evidence (Bowen et al., 2014)
- Embodied/somatic approaches: growing evidence base (van der Kolk, 2014)
